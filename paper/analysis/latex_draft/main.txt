Corporate sustainability reporting is intended to help users understand companies’ material
impacts on people and the environment, and how sustainability matters affect their business.
The 2023 European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS) articulate this purpose and
require information to be complete, neutral and accurate. They describe verifiability as the
possibility of corroborating information or the inputs used to derive it 1 . These requirements
make the connection between a disclosure and its evidence a central concern when reporting
text is assembled from multiple company sources. A statement’s usefulness depends partly on
whether its meaning survives that transformation.
Large language models have been applied to sustainability-information analysis and disclosure
drafting. ChatReport retrieves passages from corporate reports to answer questions and analyse
climate-related disclosures 2 . SusGen-GPT uses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for
sustainability report generation, incorporating information retrieved from annual reports into
drafting prompts 3 . Such workflows provide a concrete setting in which to examine the evidentiary
basis of generated reporting statements. The availability of relevant source material does not, by
itself, establish that a generated statement preserves the proposition supported by that material.
The difficulty extends beyond introducing facts absent from the source. A disclosure may
retain familiar entities, actions or numbers while changing a qualification that determines their
meaning. A description of a subsidiary’s practice, for example, does not alone establish a
group-wide practice, and evidence of an action in an earlier year does not necessarily establish
its continuation in the reporting year. Similarly, an achieved outcome and a future target remain
different propositions even when they concern the same metric. We use factual boundaries to
denote such material qualifications, including entity, time, population, metric and certainty.
Preserving them requires examining both individual facts and the relationships asserted when
evidence is combined.
Existing evaluation methods provide foundations for this assessment. FActScore decomposes
generated text into atomic facts and measures their support against a specified knowledge
source 4 ; RAGAS and RAGChecker assess the support of response statements or claims in
retrieved context 5,6 . Context preservation and combined evidence are also established concerns.

VeriScore retains necessary spatial and temporal modifiers in verifiable claims 7 , while Claimify
uses context to resolve ambiguity during claim extraction 8 . ALCE evaluates whether concatenated
cited passages support a statement 9 . These approaches motivate explicit treatment of claim
meaning and evidentiary scope when adapting support assessment to sustainability reporting.
Sustainability-specific work provides direct precedents as well. ChatReport evaluates answer
support and source attribution with human annotators and documents an erroneous concatenation of separate retrieved chunks 2 . ESG-Bench provides human annotations of support and
hallucination in ESG question answering 10 . Building on this work, the measurement question for
disclosure drafting is how to characterize support while preserving material reporting qualifiers
and distinguishing warranted inference from unsupported synthesis. An explicit account of the
evidence considered, the proposition assessed and the reason for an unsupported judgment can
make disagreements open to inspection.
Here we develop and apply Boundary-Aware Claim Evaluation (BACE) to investigate the extent,
forms and assessment of evidentiary support in AI-generated sustainability disclosures. BACE
links claims extracted from disclosures to candidate claims extracted from the evidence supplied
to the generator, distinguishes direct from inferred support, and assigns reporting-oriented
diagnoses to unsupported claims. We analyse 1,798 completed disclosures from a planned design
of 1,800 company–year–task cases spanning 30 companies, reporting years 2019–2023 and 12 tasks
based on the 2023 ESRS edition. We describe support patterns and automated diagnoses, inspect
source-linked cases, and compare assessments with the native faithfulness scores of RAGChecker
and RAGAS. The analysis concerns one fixed retrieval and generation workflow. Its judgments
are conditional on claim extraction and candidate evidence selection; they measure suppliedevidence support, not external truth or legal compliance. The contribution is a reporting-focused
operationalization and empirical comparison whose automated judgments remain subject to
independent validation.

Results
Study scope and boundary-aware evaluation
We examined the evidentiary support of corporate sustainability disclosures generated for 30
companies across five reporting years, 2019–2023, and 12 tasks based on the 2023 European
Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS). The tasks covered climate change, own workforce
and business conduct. The design specified 1,800 company–year–task cases; 1,798 had completed
BACE assessments and available scores from both comparison methods and formed the analysed
cohort (Fig. 1). The two incomplete BACE cases were excluded from support summaries
and paired comparisons. Each disclosure was generated using a fixed retrieval and generation
configuration, with narrative passages, table rows and structured metrics drawn from the
permitted company-specific source years.
Boundary-Aware Claim Evaluation (BACE) reconstructed support relationships between claims
extracted from each generated disclosure and claims extracted from its supplied evidence. A
disclosure claim received direct support when an accepted evidence set explicitly entailed its
complete meaning, and inferred support when entailment required a necessary reasoning step.
Claims with no accepted support set were classified as unsupported. Material qualifiers, including
entity, period, population, metric and certainty, formed part of the proposition being assessed.
These judgments operated on extracted claims and selected candidate evidence claims (Fig. 1);

they assessed support within the supplied evidence boundary rather than independently verified
corporate facts.

Evidentiary support across disclosure tasks
Across the 1,798 completed disclosures, the mean disclosure-level BACE support rate was 55.4%
(95% company-cluster bootstrap interval, 53.9–56.8%; Fig. 2a). This estimate gave each disclosure
equal weight and counted both direct and inferred support. Pooling claims instead yielded a
support rate of 55.1%: of 94,955 deduplicated disclosure claims, 34,430 received direct support
(36.3%), 17,879 received inferred support (18.8%) and 42,646 were classified as unsupported
(44.9%; Fig. 2b). The pooled proportions describe claims, whereas the disclosure-level mean
describes the average assessed disclosure.
Mean support varied across the reporting tasks, from 43.9% for workforce targets (S1-5) to
62.5% for business conduct policies (G1-1; Fig. 2c). The workforce-policies and workforce-targets
tasks each contributed 149 completed disclosures; the other tasks each contributed 150. These
differences describe the observed workflow and evidence configuration. They do not isolate task
difficulty or establish that one reporting topic is intrinsically more amenable to evidence-supported
generation.
The primary support denominator retained all extracted disclosure claims, including assertions
that information had not been disclosed or supplied. Excluding claims assigned the non-disclosurestatement diagnosis increased the support rate by a median of 1.9 percentage points within
disclosures, with a maximum change of 58.0 percentage points. This sensitivity analysis shows
that such assertions materially affect some disclosure-level scores. Neither denominator converts
an automated unsupported proportion into a factual-error rate.

Patterns of unsupported disclosure claims
Factual boundary distortion and evidence conflation together accounted for 31,335 of the 42,646
unsupported-claim diagnoses (73.5%; Fig. 3a). BACE assigned factual boundary distortion to
19,130 claims (44.9% of unsupported claims) and evidence conflation to 12,205 (28.6%). The
remaining diagnoses were non-disclosure statements, inferential inflation, unsupported novelty
and contradiction. Boundary distortion denotes a transfer of an anchored fact beyond a material
qualifier; conflation denotes an unsupported relationship constructed from separately evidenced
elements. These are mutually exclusive outputs of an ordered diagnostic procedure, rather than
independently established frequencies of underlying reporting errors.
A temporal example illustrates why the reporting context matters to those judgments (Fig. 3b).
In a disclosure generated for Wärtsilä’s 2022 supplier-relationships task, the statement that
the company uses an online solution for supply-chain risk identification was assigned factual
boundary distortion. The cited evidence explicitly describes such a solution in a 2019 report.
BACE disputed whether that historical description established use in the target reporting context;
the source does not establish that the company subsequently discontinued the solution. This
ambiguity is relevant to the study design, in which PDF evidence precedes the target reporting
year, while structured metrics may include that year.
Source inspection also exposed a limitation in an automated diagnosis (Fig. 3c). For ASML’s
2019 emissions disclosure, a contradiction rationale relied on an extracted evidence claim that
associated electricity-consumption conversion factors with direct Scope 1 emissions. In the

original 2018 report glossary, those factors belong to the adjacent indirect Scope 2 entry; the
direct Scope 1 entry instead refers to natural-gas combustion and purchased carbon dioxide.
Flattening the glossary had obscured the row association. This case challenges the recorded
contradiction rationale and demonstrates why diagnostic counts must be interpreted alongside
the transformations from source documents to evidence claims.

Differences among automated evaluators
The same 1,798 disclosures received mean native faithfulness scores of 90.2% from RAGChecker
and 90.7% from RAGAS, compared with BACE’s mean support rate of 55.4% (Fig. 4a). Mean
within-disclosure differences, calculated as BACE minus comparator, were −34.9 percentage
points for RAGChecker (95% company-cluster bootstrap interval, −36.2 to −33.5) and −35.3
percentage points for RAGAS (−36.5 to −34.1; Fig. 4b). These comparisons paired disclosures
but retained each method’s native claim inventory and scoring procedure.
The median numbers of claims per disclosure were 52 for BACE, 40 for RAGChecker and 42 for
RAGAS (Fig. 4c). Thus, matching the generated text and supplied evidence did not produce
identical sets of evaluated propositions. The internal checking contexts also differed: RAGChecker
checked individual evidence cards, RAGAS used their combined context, and BACE assessed
selected extracted evidence claims (Methods). Inventory size alone does not explain the score
differences, and the differences do not establish an accuracy ranking. We therefore inspected
specific native judgments against the generated passages and their complete supplied evidence.
One disagreement changed the unit being counted. In SCA’s generated 2022 corruption-andbribery disclosure, five incidents involving worker dismissal or discipline became five workers
dismissed or disciplined. The supplied indicator counted incidents, without specifying the number
of distinct workers. BACE classified the claim as unsupported and diagnosed factual boundary
distortion. Both RAGChecker and RAGAS retained the five-worker proposition in their extracted
claims and marked it supported. RAGAS’s justification itself referred to five incidents. Because
an incident count does not determine a distinct-worker count, the shared positive judgments
accepted a proposition that the supplied indicator did not establish.
A population distinction appeared in Continental’s generated 2021 workforce disclosure. One
supplied indicator reported 62% employee coverage by qualifying health and safety management
systems; another reported 62% audited or certified system coverage for own workers. The
generated passage combined these into an employee-specific audited-coverage percentage. Both
comparators accepted that proposition, whereas BACE diagnosed evidence conflation. The
supplied records contained no definition equating the two populations for this indicator. Identical
percentages did not establish the employee-specific audited-coverage rate, although they also did
not prove that the company’s actual rate differed.
A climate-related disagreement concerned the role assigned to an emissions value. Banco BPM’s
generated 2023 disclosure described a strategic plan envisaging a reduction from 17.5 thousand
to 7.4 thousand tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent. Both comparators accepted the plannedreduction interpretation; RAGChecker explicitly labelled 7.4 thousand tonnes as the target. The
supplied table row contained both numbers but had lost their column headings. Inspection of
the original 2022 report showed that 17.5 thousand tonnes was the 2019 value, 7.4 thousand
tonnes was the 2022 status, and the target column specified carbon neutrality during the plan
horizon. BACE returned unsupported, but its candidate evidence claims omitted the numerical
cells. Its verdict therefore does not demonstrate recovery of the original table structure.

These targeted cases locate disagreements in counting units, population qualifiers and the
attribution of values to outcomes or targets. They complement the aggregate comparison by
showing which propositions were accepted, while the source-extraction counterexample identifies a
limitation within BACE. The cases were selected through lexical screening followed by inspection
of complete passages and supplied evidence (Methods). They do not provide representative error
frequencies or independent human validation of any evaluator.

Methods
Study design and company selection
We evaluated one fixed retrieval and generation workflow for corporate sustainability disclosure.
The observational unit was a generated disclosure for one company, target reporting year and
task. The source register contained 600 companies; 250 met the source-availability criteria.
Eligibility required a known latest primary Sustainable Industry Classification System sector
at or before 2023, at least one extracted structured-metric record marked valid and containing
a nonempty value in every source year from 2015 to 2023, and a locally available PDF with a
valid file header in every source year from 2015 to 2022. Subsequent parsing checked annual
narrative availability. Eligibility did not depend on task-specific metrics or evaluator scores, but
restricted the study to companies with the required source coverage. The manuscript questions
and descriptive comparisons were organized after the experiment and were not preregistered
hypotheses.
We selected 30 companies across the 11 eligible sectors. One place was allocated to each sector;
the remaining 19 were allocated by Hamilton largest remainders, weighted by eligible sector
size minus one, with alphabetical tie-breaking. Within each sector, company identifiers were
sorted and shuffled using Python’s pseudorandom generator, initialized with the integer SHA-256
digest of the string comprising the sampling seed, 20260916, a colon and the sector name. The
first companies up to the sector quota were selected, with the remainder forming an ordered
reserve. The protocol permitted replacement by the next same-sector reserve if a required source
year proved unusable before generation, but prohibited replacement on generated content or
evaluation results. No replacement was recorded for this draw.

Disclosure tasks and temporal evidence boundaries
Each company was assigned 12 tasks in each target reporting year from 2019 to 2023, giving a
planned matrix of 1,800 cases. Task definitions used the 2023 European Sustainability Reporting
Standards as a common framework: climate transition plans, actions and resources, targets, and
greenhouse gas emissions (E1-1, E1-3, E1-4 and E1-6); workforce policies, actions and resources,
targets, and health and safety metrics (S1-1, S1-4, S1-5 and S1-14); and business conduct policies,

supplier relationships, anti-corruption controls, and corruption and bribery incidents (G1-1 to
G1-4). Applying these definitions to earlier reporting years specified a generation task; it did not
establish a legal obligation for each company and year.
For target year t, structured metrics were eligible from source years t − 4 through t, whereas PDF
reports were eligible from t − 4 through t − 1. These were report-year filters, not publication-date
cutoffs. Reports could describe measurements from other periods or prospective commitments,
whose temporal qualifiers remained relevant to support. The historical PDF window could
therefore limit evidence for claims about current practices, even where current-year structured
metrics were available. Disclosures generated for other tasks were not supplied as evidence.

Evidence preparation and retrieval
The evidence corpus combined structured-metric cards rendered deterministically from an existing
extraction CSV, narrative passages extracted from reports with PyMuPDF, and table rows
extracted with pdfplumber. Narrative preparation used the recorded 40–250-word chunking
rules. Eligible reports were processed without task-keyword page selection. Stable evidence
identifiers, source years, file hashes and source references were retained; PDF cards also retained
page locations. Structured-metric cards represented extracted CSV records and were not treated
as independently verified quotations from original reports. Flattening narrative and table layouts
could alter relationships between labels and values.
Before generation, a quality gate excluded an entire card if it contained at least ten unmapped
private-use or replacement glyphs comprising more than 5% of non-whitespace characters, or
exceeded 8,191 tokens under the cl100k_base tokenizer. The preparation record also identifies
four quarantined Evonik 2023 metric mappings following source inspection. Original reports
and exclusion records were retained, and source-year availability was rechecked after quarantine.
These checks addressed specified extraction problems without certifying the remaining source
representations. Missing metrics and evidence types were not imputed.
Retrieval combined BM25 with cosine similarity over 3,072-dimensional text-embedding-3-large
embeddings. Company and source-year filters were applied before ranking. Each channel retrieved
up to 200 candidates per task subquery; reciprocal rank fusion combined subquery rankings
within channels and then the channel rankings, using a rank constant of 60. Stored corpus vectors
were already normalized; the retrieval configuration disabled additional corpus normalization.
Ties were resolved by best individual rank, most recent source year and evidence identifier.
The final selection retained up to ten cards from each evidence type, without a reranker or
redistribution of unfilled quotas. Evidence entered the generation prompt in narrative, table-row
and structured-metric order. Thus, 30 cards was a maximum, not a requirement for every case.
The query bundles, selected card order and generation inputs were saved before the main run.

Disclosure generation and incomplete cases
Generation used the configured Azure OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol deployment, with recorded modelversion label 2026-07-09, medium reasoning effort, no temperature parameter and an output
budget of 8,192 tokens. The shared prompt requested a professional disclosure of 300–500 words
based on the supplied evidence. The first complete response meeting the length rule was accepted.
Incomplete or out-of-range responses triggered up to two retries of the identical prompt per
invocation, with rejected attempts retained; response selection did not use evaluator scores.

The runtime permitted at most three attempts per stage. These settings and saved outputs
document the executed workflow but do not guarantee byte-identical responses from a later
remote deployment.
Generation preceded the BACE, RAGChecker and RAGAS evaluation branches. Stage receipts
recorded dependencies and artifact hashes so that verified completed work could be reused
after interruption. Unresolved evaluations remained missing. The saved analysis contained
1,798 completed BACE cases, all with available native comparator scores. Banco BPM’s 2019
workforce-targets case and Investec’s 2023 workforce-policies case had incomplete BACE branches
and were excluded without zero imputation. Their exclusion did not imply failure of generation
or of every comparator branch. The analysed cohort and saved run status were retained explicitly
rather than treating the planned matrix as fully completed.

BACE claim construction and support judgments
BACE first constructed evidence claims (ECs) from the complete prompt-visible evidence and
disclosure claims (DCs) from the generated disclosure. The fixed sequence comprised direct
extraction version 8, identified-patch review version 4 using operational definition version 2,
constrained atomicity review version 2, and semantic deduplication within each case. The BACE
stage configurations selected Azure gpt-5.6-sol, with model-version label 2026-07-09 and medium
reasoning effort. The extraction rules required material qualifiers, including entity, period,
population, metric, unit and certainty, to remain attached to their propositions. Each initial EC
was attributed to one evidence card, and deduplication retained source-occurrence lineage. The
configured schema-only acceptance mode did not require a quoted string to match its source
exactly; stored quotation fields consequently did not establish verified attribution.
Candidate selection operated on the deduplicated EC and DC texts. It did not receive retrieval
scores, task definitions or separate source-provenance fields as additional factual premises. For
each DC, the support judge received the DC text, selected candidate EC identifiers and texts,
company name and target reporting year. It did not receive the original evidence cards or the
full unselected EC inventory at this stage. Company and year provided interpretive context but
could not supply absent facts. Support decisions were therefore conditional on the preceding
extraction and candidate selection.
The support prompt requested every distinct minimal sufficient EC set that supported the
complete DC, including its material qualifiers. Minimality meant that removing any member
would leave that set insufficient; it did not require choosing only the smallest set among all
alternatives. A set was direct when it explicitly entailed the claim, allowing equivalent wording,
and inferred when entailment required a necessary reasoning step rather than a merely plausible
assumption. Support type did not depend on the number of ECs in a set. Any accepted direct
set produced a direct DC verdict; accepted inferred sets without a direct set produced an inferred
verdict; no accepted set produced an unsupported verdict. An empty candidate set yielded an
empty support result without a support-model call. The output contained support sets and
their types, rather than a prose support rationale. Structural validation checked identifiers and
record completeness, but did not independently establish the semantic validity, minimality or
exhaustiveness of the returned sets.
Each unsupported DC received one diagnosis, assigned in a fixed precedence order. A nondisclosure statement asserted an absence of disclosure or evidence within a specified scope.
Contradiction required an incompatible proposition at matching material boundaries. Evidence

conflation combined elements from multiple ECs into an unsupported relationship or attribution.
Factual boundary distortion transferred an anchored fact across a material qualifier, such as entity,
time, population, metric or unit. Inferential inflation strengthened meaning, status, certainty or
conclusion beyond the evidence while retaining the relevant factual boundaries. Unsupported
novelty applied when no candidate EC anchored the core proposition. These mutually exclusive
outputs characterized the automated diagnostic procedure. Assertions about absent information
entered support assessment first and were not automatically assigned an unsupported diagnosis.

Diagnosis proportions used all unsupported DCs as their denominator. The metric implementation
required nonempty EC and DC inventories, complete support records and diagnoses that exactly
partitioned unsupported DCs; invalid or incomplete cases did not receive fabricated scores.
For sensitivity analysis, Zd denoted the number of unsupported DCs diagnosed as non-disclosure
statements. The alternative rate was (Ad +Id )/(Nd −Zd ), and its change from sd was summarized
within disclosures in percentage points. This removed only claims assigned that diagnosis.
Additional saved metrics were evidence-claim coverage, defined as the number of distinct ECs
appearing in any accepted support set divided by the total deduplicated EC inventory, and
inference rate, defined as Id /(Ad + Id ). Zero denominators produced missing values. Inference
rate differed from the inferred share of all DCs reported in Results; coverage described use of
supplied extracted evidence, not completeness of corporate reporting.
RAGChecker evaluates claim-level entailment and defines faithfulness through support in retrieved
context 6 . RAGAS assesses faithfulness by extracting response statements and checking their
support in the supplied context 5 . We used RAGChecker 0.1.9 with RefChecker 0.2.13 and
RAGAS 0.4.3. A common adapter supplied the same generated response and ordered promptvisible evidence, checking prompt, response and context hashes. Each comparator retained its
native extraction prompts, checking procedure and claim denominator. Only faithfulness was
evaluated; no reference answers were supplied. The configured Azure gpt-5.6-sol judge used
medium reasoning. The transport adapter supplied these reasoning and output-budget settings
in place of package-default request parameters while preserving native prompts and scoring.
In the recorded RAGChecker procedure, extracted response triplets were checked against individual evidence cards; a claim counted as supported if at least one card received an entailment
verdict. The joint-checking group size was five claims, not five merged evidence cards. RAGAS
checked its extracted statements against the combined supplied contexts and calculated their
supported fraction. Invalid extraction, response or statement-alignment outputs were subject to

the recorded retry policy and remained missing if unresolved. Matching source inputs at the
adapter level did not align the evaluated propositions or make either comparator’s checking
context identical to BACE’s candidate ECs. Native score differences could therefore reflect
multiple stages and did not isolate evaluator accuracy.

Aggregation and uncertainty
We reported disclosure-level and task-level means, pooled claim counts and diagnosis composition
using the completed cohort. Paired comparisons used the intersection of available BACE,
RAGChecker and RAGAS scores. For each comparator, its score was subtracted from BACE’s
support rate within each disclosure before averaging; differences were expressed in percentage
points. Native claim-count medians used the same paired disclosures. No sector population
weights were applied, and claims from different native inventories were not treated as matched
measurement units.
The saved analysis used 2,000 company-cluster bootstrap draws with NumPy’s default random
generator and seed 20260921. Each draw sampled companies with replacement and retained
every observed disclosure from each sampled company, including multiplicities. For each mean
or paired difference, the statistic divided the sum of the resampled disclosure values by their
resampled count. Thus, clustering accommodated repeated years and tasks within companies
while preserving disclosure weighting. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles formed the reported 95%
intervals. Task-specific calculations used the observed disclosures for that task. The analysis was
descriptive and did not perform significance tests. These intervals did not include uncertainty
from additional model runs, extraction, human annotation or unresolved cases, and did not
implement a sector-design-adjusted extrapolation to the register.

Selection and source inspection of illustrative cases
The fixed diagnostic illustrations originated in within-label candidate pools that required a
localizable quotation in the generated text. The Wärtsilä temporal case and ASML contradiction
case used the recorded selection seeds 20260924 and 20260922, respectively. These case identifiers
were retained during manuscript preparation. Source inspection assessed whether their particular
rationales preserved the evidence’s temporal or tabular relationships; it did not validate the
complete disclosures. The ASML example was retained because the original glossary challenged
the recorded contradiction rationale.
Additional comparator disagreements were discovered through targeted screening of saved outputs.
The initial screen considered BACE contradictions and other unsupported DCs with an exact
normalized match to a supported RAGAS statement. It then identified the nearest native
claims by token-set Jaccard overlap and retained candidates with positive judgments from both
comparators. A supplementary screen searched unsupported quantitative DCs containing a digit
and a specified unit or status keyword, excluded non-disclosure statements, and required both
nearest native claims to be supported with Jaccard overlap of at least 0.65. Exact normalization
used Unicode NFKC, case folding and word-token joining. These lexical operations retrieved
candidates; they did not establish semantic equivalence or representative error frequencies.
For the selected SCA, Continental and Banco BPM cases, inspection compared the complete
generated passage, all supplied evidence cards, the disputed DC, its candidate ECs and the exact
native statements and verdicts. Candidates were not promoted as clear shared disagreements

when a native statement omitted the disputed qualifier, another supplied card established support,
or the complete passage qualified the apparent overstatement. The original Banco BPM report
was inspected after evaluation to resolve column headings missing from the supplied table
row; those restored headings were not evaluator inputs. Its BACE candidate ECs omitted the
numerical cells, so an unsupported verdict did not demonstrate recovery of the original structure.
These source-linked inspections were part of manuscript preparation, with AI assistance, and
were not independent human annotation or estimates of evaluator accuracy.

Prepared independent human assessment
An independent assessment package was prepared to examine BACE judgments conditional on
their actual DC and candidate EC inputs. The formal design documented on 25 September
2026 replaced the broader source-review scope described in the original protocol. It selected
600 unique DCs from the completed inventory: 400 primary units, allocated proportionally
across automated direct, inferred and unsupported strata using largest remainders, and 200
supplementary units from the remaining unsupported pool. Primary quotas were 145, 75 and 180,
respectively. Selection used seeded SHA-256 ranking without replacement, with seed bace-humanvalidation-v1-20260924. Supplementary allocation successively favoured the least represented
diagnosis, breaking ties in the fixed diagnostic order and capping total contradiction units at 40
to retain calibration-eligible units. An independent seeded ranking set the presentation order,
with group membership withheld from reviewer workbooks.
Two workbooks contained the same 600 sample identifiers and supplied company name, target
year, complete DC text and the exact candidate EC membership, order, identifiers and wording.
Automated answers were omitted, and the instructions required independent review without
access to the other reviewer’s answers. As of the local file check on 1 October 2026, both
workbooks had blank support and diagnosis cells; the manifest recorded prepared, not annotated,
and calibration had not been conducted. No reviewer agreement, adjudication or accuracy
estimates were available from these files. This status describes the inspected local copies and
does not establish whether completed annotations exist elsewhere.
The prepared design specifies primary-stratum weights for overall estimates from the 400 primary
units. Supplementary inclusion probabilities are conditional on the realized primary sample; the
combined 600 units must not be analysed as an unweighted representative sample. Reviewers
are to assign direct, inferred or unsupported labels and one diagnosis only when selecting
unsupported. Any future calibration must use separate units. The supplied material permits
assessment of conditional support and diagnosis decisions, but not independent validation of
extraction, candidate selection, original-source completeness, real-world truth or comparative
evaluator accuracy. Reviewer qualifications, completed calibration, agreement procedures and
adjudication outcomes require documentation before reporting human-assessment results.

Figure 1: Study design and evidence boundaries. a, The planned matrix comprises 30
companies, five reporting years (2019–2023) and 12 disclosure tasks. Of 1,800 planned cases,
1,798 have completed BACE assessments and both native comparator scores; two incomplete
BACE cases are excluded without zero imputation. For target reporting year t, PDF reports
supply narrative and table evidence from t − 4 through t − 1, while extracted metric records cover
t − 4 through t. These are source-year filters, not publication-date cutoffs. Retrieval supplies
up to ten cards per evidence type, with a maximum of 30. b, Solid arrows trace automated
processing. Evidence claims (ECs) and disclosure claims (DCs) are constructed by extraction,
review and deduplication. Candidate selection uses EC and DC texts. Support assessment
receives one DC, its selected candidate ECs, company and reporting year, and assigns direct,
inferred or unsupported verdicts. Only unsupported DCs receive one of six ordered diagnoses.
The dashed amber branch marks prepared human assessment using the same judgment inputs:
two reviewer workbooks contain the same 600 sampled DCs, with annotation cells blank in
the inspected local copies. Support judgments remain conditional on extraction and candidate
selection; neither those judgments nor the prepared review establishes external corporate truth.
