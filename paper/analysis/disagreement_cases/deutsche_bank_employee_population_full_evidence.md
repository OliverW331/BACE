### S1-1 – Policies related to own workforce

Deutsche Bank’s policy framework for its own workforce is centred on diversity and inclusion, equal opportunity, human rights, and responsible working conditions. As a global organisation, the bank is committed to fostering an inclusive culture that respects and embraces the diversity of its employees, clients and communities. These principles support an environment in which employees can act responsibly and have the opportunity to realise their potential.

The Diversity & Inclusion agenda addresses gender equality, cultural and generational diversity, equal treatment of employees with disabilities, and equal opportunities for lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and intersex employees. The bank’s position is that no person should be disadvantaged because of their sexual orientation. Employee-led initiatives, including dbPride, support awareness and inclusion for LGBTI employees. The bank has also sought to increase wider workforce awareness of diversity and an inclusive working environment.

The values, beliefs and implementation measures associated with Deutsche Bank’s understanding of diversity and inclusion apply, to the extent legally admissible, to the Supervisory Board and Management Board of Deutsche Bank AG. The Supervisory Board considers diversity within the company when filling positions on both boards. This embeds oversight of diversity considerations at the highest governance level and links the workforce policy approach to leadership appointments.

The policy framework is informed by internationally recognised principles. Deutsche Bank participates in the Ten Principles of the UN Global Compact and the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights and has published a dedicated Human Rights Statement. The bank has also reported alignment with International Labour Organization standards and the OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises. These external frameworks provide reference points for its approach to human rights and labour-related matters.

In 2020, the reported employee population comprised 84,659 employees. The process relating to the assessment of material impacts, risks and opportunities for the own-workforce topic was last modified on October 30, 2020. Workforce-related management arrangements also included health and safety systems: in 2019, 92.2% of employees were reported as covered by a health and safety management system based on legal requirements and/or recognised standards or guidelines.

The reported policy commitments are primarily directional and principle-based. The available 2020 information does not specify new quantitative targets for diversity, inclusion, human rights or health and safety, nor does it describe time-bound objectives or associated progress measures for these policy areas.
