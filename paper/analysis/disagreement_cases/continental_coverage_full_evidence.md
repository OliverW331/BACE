### S1-4 – Actions, resources and effectiveness related to our own workforce

Continental’s approach to good working conditions is based on its Code of Conduct, which establishes the principles for global collaboration and includes requirements concerning human rights, fair working conditions and equal opportunities. Our actions focus particularly on employability, workforce transformation, occupational health and safety, and flexible working arrangements.

To manage changes in workforce requirements, Continental applies strategic workforce planning. This methodology compares the long-term development of the existing workforce with future quantitative and qualitative personnel requirements, enabling the identification of skills gaps, capacity gaps and overlaps and the definition of appropriate HR measures. In connection with the Transformation 2019–2029 structural program, a preliminary analysis indicated that up to 20,000 jobs worldwide could be affected over ten years. Continental’s response includes structured retraining intended to support continued employability and prepare affected employees for technological change and associated professional and personal challenges. Where necessary, feasible and legally permissible, employees are offered opportunities elsewhere in the corporate network, including through the internal job market.

Training and development resources include local programs for production employees worldwide, addressing technical qualifications, occupational safety and product quality, supplemented by further training, qualification and development programs at individual locations. Continental performs regular checks of the effectiveness and sustainability of these measures. Talent management processes have also included conferences covering more than 80,000 salaried employees, in which individual potential and development needs were assessed and corresponding measures defined. Flexible-working arrangements adopted in 2016 provided an established basis for remote work during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Occupational health and safety are supported through governance and specialist resources. At corporate level, technical responsibility lies with the Safety and Health department, which reports directly to the Executive Board member responsible for Human Relations. In Germany, company or site-level occupational health and safety agreements and statutory occupational health and safety committees provide for participation by works council members, employer representatives, company doctors, occupational health and safety specialists and safety representatives. Internationally, applicable legal occupational safety structures are observed. Continental has also pursued certification of occupational safety management systems and migration to ISO 45001.

In 2021, 62% of employees were covered by a health and safety management system based on legal requirements and/or recognized standards or guidelines. The same proportion was covered by systems that had been internally audited and/or externally audited or certified. This compares with 69% coverage reported for 2020 and provides a measure of the reach and assurance of the management approach. Continental’s process concerning material own-workforce matters was last modified on December 31, 2021, with the next materiality reassessment scheduled for 2023.
